# StudBud

StudBud is a student hub with three parts: **Plan** (classes, assignments, grades, calendar), **Study** (flashcards scheduled with Anki's SM-2 spaced-repetition algorithm, focus sessions, study streaks and leaderboards) and **Play** (live multiplayer games and arcade modes powered by your own flashcards, with a coin shop for characters, hats and accessories). Flashcard study shuffles due reviews and new cards within daily limits; future reviews wait until their scheduled day. Decks show how many cards are scheduled today; when nothing is due, Study offers a Cram run instead. Learning/relearning steps, Again/Hard/Good/Easy interval adjustments and leech detection remain active. Quiz and game questions are randomized independently.

## Open StudBud

After the GitHub Pages deployment finishes, open **https://mystudbud.org/** in Chrome, Safari, Edge, or Firefox. The site works on phones, tablets, Chromebooks, and computers without installing anything.

On Chrome for desktop, use the install icon in the address bar or choose **Install StudBud** from the browser menu. On Android, open the site in Chrome and choose **Add to Home screen**. On iPhone or iPad, open it in Safari and choose **Share → Add to Home Screen**.

The app shell is cached for offline loading after the first visit. Internet access is needed for account sign-in and syncing, externally hosted fonts and icons, and chart libraries.

## Ads

Optional, non-sticky banner slots sit at the bottom of Home, the decks list, and the shop. They stay collapsed until configured and are hidden offline, during tutorials, study sessions, games, and dialogs. There are no interstitials, rewarded ads, videos, or automatic refreshes.

In `ads.js`, fill in `window.STUDBUD_ADS.client` with your AdSense publisher ID (`ca-pub-XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX`) and `slots.home`, `slots.decks`, and/or `slots.shop` with the corresponding ad-unit slot IDs. Leave any unused slot empty. The Google script loads only when a configured banner enters the viewport. Use `window.StudBudAds.refresh()` after changing configuration at runtime. Ad blockers or network errors leave the app usable.

Replace the commented example in `ads.txt` with your AdSense-authorized seller line and publish it at the **domain root** (for project-path hosting, this may require the root Pages repository or a custom domain). Follow AdSense approval, privacy/consent, and child-directed-content requirements before enabling ads; this integration does not supply a consent-management platform.

### Game regression checks

River Fishing now has fewer rocks, logs break when they strike rocks, and whirlpools pull boats into a spinning spiral before ejecting them. Package Delivery uses lighter traffic, fixed road-only car lanes, narrow sidewalks, larger collision bounds and safe recesses at all four building corners. Crystal Mining keeps most natural caves dry and distinguishes their teal rims from brown mined tunnels; tile details are precomputed, offscreen lava effects are skipped, and settled lava stops updating until nearby mining wakes it.

King of the Hill's special charges from five seconds of uncontested hill control or three landed attacks, costs 10 energy and has a 12-second cooldown. It hits at triple power over a 180-unit reach, with a charge meter, ready ring and larger attack effects. Arena Shooter has a larger map, moderately relaxed camera edges, clearer weapon silhouettes and an eased, labeled weapon-scope zoom.

Team Tower Defense is a separate arcade mode; the existing study Tower Defense remains available. Two players form a 1v1, while larger rosters alternate between blue and red teams. Each side starts with 100 base HP and a shared $180 wallet. Walk anywhere with WASD or the joystick; choose an Archer, Cannon, Frost, Ballista, Tesla or Venom tower and click/tap an empty grid cell on your own side to build. Placement snaps to 70-unit cells, with a valid/invalid cell highlight, tower ghost and full range preview. Each player can own up to **12 towers**, independently of teammates; there is no 36-tower team cap. Teammates can still upgrade one another's towers using the shared wallet, without changing ownership. Select a friendly tower to buy upgrades through level 3 and see its damage, range and firing-speed changes. The two-page catalog includes Scouts, Runners, Brutes, Swarms, armored Sentinels and Wisps. Ballista bolts ignore armor, Tesla chains hit three targets, and Venom applies damage over time. Enemies enter the opposing team's marked path. Correct flashcard answers earn three times the room's configured answer reward for the shared wallet; defeated enemies pay the defending team a bounty. There is no passive income or time limit: the first base to reach zero HP loses. Chess and Soccer gameplay are unchanged.

Tower Defense placement keeps the camera steady when the pointer moves. Tower selection uses the whole grid cell; clicking empty ground with a selected tower only deselects it, and Escape also clears selection. HUD controls and the minimap block world placement using their current rendered bounds.

City Escape spans a 20,000-unit-wide world in the order **City → Factory → Space**, with exactly three visible biome entrance checkpoints and no hidden rooftop respawn anchors. City starts in an alley on a short three-flight fire escape that puts you on a low first roof, then runs across a long line of connected skyscrapers. Each run plays a seeded order of distinct crossings (rooftop hops, wall-fixture climbs, a blade-sign scale, a street scaffold, rooftop billboard catwalks, a tower crane swinging two steel beams, springs, rail carts, a window-washer gondola, a broken skybridge and a drop), never two wall climbs back to back. Roofs get alternating themes, with occasional security roofs (vertical lasers) or tripwire roofs (knee-high horizontal lasers), and the city ends on a fire escape into the factory. Factory and Space are built from chambers of hand-mapped set pieces, each used once per biome, so no section repeats. Consecutive sections avoid sharing machinery. Factory sections include a belt gauntlet of three long conveyors (against you, then carrying you forward, then against you again) where you time each run past phased crushers and drills, an assembly line with transfer gaps and smashers, a stamping feed, freight elevators, catapults, pistons and crumbling gantries. Space is a station with airlock halls, docking arms, solar walks, truss climbs, thruster boosts, debris showers and a climb hopping between moving meteors. Asteroids spawn off screen on fixed lanes, cross once and despawn, so every pass is a new rock. Tap Jump for a short hop, press again for a double jump, and use Dash for longer transfers. Curved/straight slope collision follows the visible surface across the runner's full foot width. Walking into lips of 10 px or less steps onto them, jumps clipping a ceiling corner by 8 px or less slide past, and moving platforms use previous/current-position relative sweeps with collision-resolved carry, including vertical reversals, upward landings and low-frame-rate side contacts. Carry stops at walls instead of dragging riders through them; jumping releases the deck immediately, descending machinery resolves head contact vertically, and genuinely trapped solid-platform riders respawn rather than teleporting through a ceiling. Camera-local collision/drawing and nearby hazard simulation keep full-detail play bounded, while passed checkpoint areas are retired. Timed/crumbling platforms flash before disappearing; inactive landings show faint dashed outlines.

City routes favor taller 7-10-rung climbs and 1,000-1,250-unit wall-jump shafts. Its larger circular sun has a solid center and a short, subtle edge fade, with no outline or rays. Each biome checkpoint saves automatically when the runner's feet reach its height anywhere across the map; flags indicate safe respawn positions, not touch targets. Backgrounds begin fading once the next biome entrance is visibly inside the camera (18% of the viewport height, capped at 120 world units), then crossfade over just 240 camera units. Factory cogs do not appear while the factory entrance is still offscreen. Platforms and cargo rails attach to a shared steel frame. Background machinery is connected cog trains, shafts and drive belts, without cabinet-like panels. Timed broad crushers and drills warn before slamming onto production decks, leaving escape margins. Space has several planet palettes, rings, moons, satellites and asteroid/station/thruster platform designs. ## Import from Infinite Campus

Use **Classes → Import from Infinite Campus** (also in **Settings → Data Management**) to bring in this school year's classes, grade categories and weights, all graded and upcoming assignments, class periods and times, and the school calendar.

1. On a computer, drag the **StudBud Import** button to your bookmarks bar (one time only; press Ctrl+Shift+B if the bar is hidden). On a phone/tablet, open the phone instructions, choose **Copy bookmark URL**, bookmark any page as **StudBud Import**, then edit that bookmark and replace its entire address with the copied URL, including `javascript:`.
2. Choose **Open Infinite Campus** and sign in. PCHS students get the Parkway Campus address automatically; anyone else can paste their own under **Different Campus address?**.
3. On the Campus website, run the saved **StudBud Import** bookmark (or, on desktop, copy the console script and paste it into the browser console). On Safari, choose the bookmark from Bookmarks. On Chrome for Android, type its name in the address bar and tap the saved bookmark suggestion, not a search result. Use a normal browser tab, not the Campus app or an in-app browser. It reads your data in your own signed-in session (StudBud never sees your password) and sends it back to StudBud when tab communication is available. Otherwise, tap **Copy data** in the helper and **Paste data from Campus** in StudBud. If clipboard access is blocked, choose **Show data**, select/copy it manually, and paste into StudBud's data box; downloading the .json and choosing that file also works.
4. Review the preview. Each class is matched to the closest PCHS catalog course (course number first, then name); change any match, keep the Campus name, or skip it. Then choose what to import.

The app tour introduces this importer at step 3, before dashboard explanations and manual class setup, and the About page lists it. Mobile setup instructions open automatically on touch/narrow screens.

Importing again updates the same classes and assignments instead of duplicating them, and keeps your study estimates and priorities. Imported class times, school days off, and day notes appear on the dashboard schedule and calendar. Remove them from the Schedule page at any time; your class periods stay saved.

## Accounts and cloud sync

New assignments default their due date to today in your local time zone. You can type in the date field or use its single built-in calendar icon. Adding from a calendar day keeps that selected day; editing an assignment preserves its saved date. Save & add another keeps your chosen date for quick batch entry.

Score and Out of arrows (including the keyboard's up/down arrows) adjust by one point. Type fractional scores directly; decimals remain valid and are saved to two decimal places.

StudBud uses Supabase Auth for username/password accounts and stores each account's full app backup (classes, assignments, grades, schedules, flashcards, study history, and UI preferences) in a row protected by Row Level Security. Passwords must be at least 8 characters. Usernames are unique, case-insensitive, and limited to 3–24 letters, numbers, or underscores.

To connect the deployed static site to a Supabase project:

1. Create a Supabase project and run [`supabase-setup.sql`](./supabase-setup.sql) in the Supabase SQL editor.
   **End of beta:** on the existing project, also run [`beta-reset.sql`](./beta-reset.sql) once, after the setup script. It resets every account's study time, streaks, XP and level, coins (back to 100), cosmetics and daily rewards. Accounts, courses, grades, flashcard decks, friends, classes and game wins are kept. Rerunning it does nothing. The app also drops study sessions from before 2026-10-15 on every device.
2. Set the project's minimum password length to 8 in its Auth password/security settings.
3. Copy the project URL and **publishable/anon key** into `url` and `anonKey` in [`supabase-config.js`](./supabase-config.js). These are public browser credentials; never put a service-role key in this file.
4. In Supabase **Authentication → Sign In / Providers → Email**, enable email/password authentication and turn **off Confirm email**. Supabase's Email provider remains enabled, but StudBud internally maps each username to a non-deliverable `@accounts.studbud.invalid` identifier. Users do not enter an email and no confirmation email is sent.

Because accounts have no email address, there is no email-based password recovery. Users who forget their username or password cannot recover that account; keep credentials safe. Do not put personal data in the username.

The first account used on a device adopts any existing local StudBud data into its cloud account. After sign-out, the local app cache is cleared; if a different account is later used and has no cloud backup, it starts with a clean app rather than adopting the previous account's data. Signed-in changes sync automatically; sign in on another device to load the same account data. Keep using **Export / Import Backup** for an independent backup.

Cloud sync merges independent edits against the last saved baseline and retries concurrent server writes instead of replacing another device's changes. Pending local edits survive reload. Conflicting edits to the same field are reported explicitly; export a backup before resolving them. Sign-out waits for a successful save. Restoring decks, history and metrics uses one IndexedDB transaction so a failed restore does not partially clear those stores. Your own published decks are recovered automatically into your collection when opening your account.

### Offline access and compact workspaces

Open the app once online (HTTPS or localhost) to cache its shell, scripts, icon fonts and public rendering libraries. After that, it loads without a network connection; stalled asset requests fall back to the cache after six seconds.

### Guest mode (accounts are optional)

Choose **Continue as guest** on the sign-in screen to use StudBud without an account. Classes, assignments, schedules, flashcards, answer images, attachments, practice tests and solo games work fully and save on the device. When online, guests can also browse and copy community sets, view leaderboards and host or join multiplayer games (up to 3 open rooms at a time) using a private Supabase anonymous session. Guests pick a player color instead of buying cosmetics. Coins, daily rewards, shop purchases, buds, publishing sets, public profiles and leaderboard entries need an account; these are blocked both in the app and by SQL guards (`studbud_is_guest()`). Choose **Create free account** at any time: the guest's local decks and progress move into the new account. The choice persists across reloads. Without a connection, guest mode works as offline mode. This is device-local access, not offline authentication or admin authorization. Export a backup and sign out **while online** before sharing a device.

**Deployment:** rerun the full updated [`supabase-setup.sql`](./supabase-setup.sql) and enable **Anonymous Sign-ins** in Supabase Authentication (Sign In / Providers). Without it, guests still have every on-device feature; online guest features show a clear message.

Edits and study history stay in local storage/IndexedDB. Cached accounts reconnect against the last sync baseline; first-time account connections merge local-only records rather than discarding offline decks. Same-field conflicts still require the existing explicit cloud/device/backup resolution. Shared-set edits made offline are queued and retried after account connection; later edits or deletion during publication cannot be overwritten by its acknowledgement. The service worker caches public shell/library assets only, never Supabase account/API responses. A fresh device needs an initial online load; `file:` URLs do not support service-worker offline installation.

Navigation now has six primary workspaces: **Home, School, Plan, Study, Play and Settings**. Tabs group related grades/analytics, tasks/calendar/schedule, sets/practice/import, games/buds/leaderboards/shop, and settings tools. Existing routes, class links and tutorial steps remain available.

### Look and feel

StudBud has its own rounded, chunky style: the default **StudBud** color scheme, the self-hosted **Nunito** typeface, pill-style workspace tabs and pressable 3D buttons. Older saved schemes still work and can be changed in **Settings → Appearance**. Games always retain full visual quality, including on older devices and when loading legacy quality preferences. Performance improvements come from viewport culling, bounded particle storage, cached scenery and spatial collision indexing rather than automatic visual downgrades.

### Parkway schools and course catalogs

Sign-up and **Settings** offer **Parkway Central, North, South and West High School** plus **Other / not listed**. Each Parkway school has its own public school tag, and the class search only suggests courses from that school's SchooLinks catalog. District-wide data (grade scale, graduation requirements, Latin honors, school registry and catalog builder) lives in `parkwayData.js`; each school's course list lives in its own file under `data/schools/` (`central.js`, `north.js`, `south.js`, `west.js`), generated from SchooLinks. The old `window.PCHS_*` globals remain as aliases of the new `PARKWAY_*` / `STUDBUD_COURSE_CATALOG` names. Choosing **Other** replaces the search with a free-text class name; Infinite Campus import works for every school.

### Classes for teachers and students

Accounts with the **teacher** tag (assigned in Staff tools) at a Parkway school see a **Classes** workspace. Teachers pick the courses they teach from their school's catalog. There are no join codes: any student at the same school who has that course in their course list (added by hand or imported from Infinite Campus) is added automatically and sees the teacher's flashcard sets, and students who drop the course drop out of the class. Teachers see every student's grades, missing work and study time, can remove someone who isn't really in their class (and restore them later), and publish flashcard sets to the class. Teacher classes are not available for **Other** schools. **Deployment:** rerun the updated [`supabase-setup.sql`](./supabase-setup.sql) to create the classroom tables and policies.

### Flashcard tools

- **Focus mode:** Study opens a full-screen session with a progress bar, combo counter and keyboard shortcuts: Space flips, 1–4 rate, **S** stars the card and **T** switches to typing the answer (graded with the card's answer rules).
- **Cram and starred drills:** Cram goes through every card in an adaptive order without changing the spaced-repetition schedule; the star button drills only starred cards.
- **Session summary:** cards, accuracy, best streak, time and a rating breakdown, with one-tap **Review missed**.
- **Deck cards:** a mastery ring plus counts for cards, due, starred and weak cards. Class/test links, Share and Delete live under **More**.
- **Editor:** **Quick add** (term, Tab, definition, Enter), per-card stars and a Starred filter.

### Practice games

Streak Builder, Lightning Round, Survival, True or False and Memory Match use large answer tiles with 1–4 keyboard shortcuts (T/F in True or False), combo points, a streak flame, hearts in Survival and a draining timer bar in Lightning Round, where correct answers advance automatically. Every round ends on a results card with up to three stars, accuracy, points and best streak.

### Shop boxes

Boxes are flat 2D open boxes: the lid is knocked off and the rarest reward you have not collected yet peeks over the rim.

The opening reel uses the server-selected reward for its winning tile, result card, and inventory. It measures the tile and marker positions to keep the landing correct on narrow screens and after resizing; duplicate rewards report the server-provided coin refund. Run `node tests/mystery-box.cjs` for landing and result regressions, and `node tests/ads.cjs` for lazy banner, offline, and distraction-suppression checks.

### Profile hub, seasonal boxes and reporting

**Profile** is its own workspace with three tabs: **Profile** (public profile and account card), **Shop** and **Locker** (inventory and equipped cosmetics). The shop features a limited **seasonal box** each month (New Year in January through Christmas in December). Each box draws from its own cosmetic set, never gives duplicates, and leaves at the end of its month. **Seasonal sets** shows every holiday set and what you've collected. Opening any box plays a full-screen unboxing (shake, reel, reveal) that you can skip; reduced-motion settings shorten it. Guests see a **Sign in** button next to **Create account** on the guest banner. Default (no-cosmetics) players get a distinct random color in each match so they're easy to tell apart.

Signed-in students can **Report** a shared flashcard set or a public profile (inappropriate, wrong answers, spam, copyright, harassment or other, with optional details). Reports are rate-limited, and you can't report your own content. Moderators and admins review grouped reports in **Admin ? Reports**, where they can inspect the set, open the author's profile, delete the set, or mark the report handled or dismissed.

### Planner status

Every assignment is in one of four states: **To do**, **Overdue**, **Done, waiting for grade** and **Graded**. Set the state when adding the assignment. Each row shows a status pill with a one-click **Mark done**, **Add grade** or **Undo**, and the summary chips above the list filter by state with live counts. Adding an assignment always shows the class picker (preselected when you have one class); if you have no classes yet, StudBud opens the class form first.

### Game summaries and mining

End-of-game reviews group repeated questions into one row showing your accuracy on that question. Multiple-choice options are reshuffled and drawn from a varied distractor pool each time a card appears, so you can't memorize the answer position. **Question Chess** is a two-player real-time checkmate variant. Each correct question earns exactly **one move**, with at most one ready move held at once. There are **no regular alternating turns, energy settings, match timer or active-match expiry**: either player may move when ready, except that **only the checked player can move until escaping check**. Kings cannot be captured, and moving into check is illegal. Checkmate wins; stalemate or only kings remaining draws. Captured material starts at zero and uses standard values: pawn **1**, knight/bishop **3**, rook **5**, queen **9**. Winning is ranked separately from material points; draws do not select a material winner. Castling requires unmoved king/rook, clear squares and a safe king path. En passant is available immediately after the opponent's double pawn move, expiring on the next board move. Automatic queen promotion applies. The green-and-cream board uses one original, matching vector piece set for both colors and mode previews, with off-white and charcoal fills, dark outlines and subtle shading. Coordinates, last-move and check highlights, legal-move dots and capture rings clarify play. Piece movement animates unless reduced motion is enabled. A textured green gradient fills the game area, with responsive desktop, portrait and landscape layouts below the HUD. Keyboard/tap selection and mouse/touch drag moves are supported. Canceling an invalid drag preserves its credit. Solo practice has one simulated opponent.

The host serializes chess requests, validates moves against the current board and persists revisioned snapshots. A simultaneous move based on an older board is rejected without spending its credit. Repeated network requests are acknowledged only once; guests accept board snapshots only from the host and ignore older revisions. The host must remain connected; failed persistence or missing host acknowledgements are explicitly reported. Explicitly leaving ends a live chess room; finished rooms return to normal cleanup expiry. **Rerun the full updated [`supabase-setup.sql`](./supabase-setup.sql)** to enable the chess mode, two-player room limit, untimed rules, host-only board persistence and public study-set references in game review.

## Deployment

Every push to `main` automatically deploys the site to GitHub Pages using the workflow in `.github/workflows/pages.yml`. If a deployment does not start, open the repository's **Actions** tab, select **Deploy StudBud to GitHub Pages**, and choose **Run workflow** on `main`.
